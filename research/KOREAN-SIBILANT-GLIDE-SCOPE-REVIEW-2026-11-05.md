Scope: 27 candidate occurrences outside the currently accepted Kang (2010) /i, ye, ya/ profile scope.

## Result

No additional occurrence is closed by this review. The 27 lenis-sibilant occurrences remain `NOT_INDIVIDUALLY_ADJUDICATED`:

- ID 606: `쇼`, with /s/ before /jo/.
- 26 occurrences with /s/ before /jʌ/: IDs 843, 1155, 1157, 1414–1477 (22 repetitions in one source row), and 2489.

## Evidence inspected

1. Kang, Kyung-Shim (2009), *A Phonetic Account of Korean Affricates: Neutralization with a Palatal Glide*, Language Sciences 16(1), 169–186. KCI record: https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART001319180. The accessible abstract reports that Korean alveolar fricatives differ acoustically and in identification when followed by /j/. It does not identify the tested following vowel sequence, speaker count, effect size, or a categorical narrow IPA outcome. It establishes that /j/ can affect fricative realization, but does not license [ɕ] for every /jV/ context.

2. Kang, Hyunsook (2010), *Coarticulation effects of Korean sibilant /s/ before a high front segment*, DOI: https://doi.org/10.17959/sppm.2010.16.1.3. The abstract reports palatalization/coarticulation in /i, ye, ya/ contexts. It also says /yu, yo/ show a distinct spectral pattern. The evidence is acoustic coarticulation, not a categorical segmental IPA prescription.

3. Kang, Hyunsook (2010), *Korean Sibilant /s/ before a High Front and a Round Segment*, KCI/DBpia record: https://www.dbpia.co.kr/journal/articleDetail?nodeId=NODE01571069. The abstract describes a labio-palatalized spectral pattern when high-front and rounding gestures co-occur within a syllable. It does not prescribe [ɕ] or [ɕʷ] as a deterministic transcription.

## Decision and limits

- Do not generalize the 2010 /i, ye, ya/ result to /jʌ/.
- Do not treat the distinct /yo/ or labio-palatalized patterns as proof of [ɕ] for ID 606.
- Do not change the broad IPA profile, narrow-profile runtime, Ukrainian target, benchmark gold, or production phonology.
- The project broad profile may retain phonological /s/ while token-specific narrow realization remains unknown.
- Required to reopen: full-text methods/stimuli that identify the exact /jʌ/ and /jo/ environments, plus either token-level audio or independent Korean IPA annotation. A general abstract about /j/ is not enough.
